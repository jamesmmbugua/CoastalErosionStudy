Climate-smart MPA management

Bridging the gap between climate data and reef management decisions

CliMPA translates essential climate variables into decision-relevant information for the managers responsible for Marine Protected Areas in the Western Indian Ocean, starting with the Mafia Island Marine Park (MIMP), Tanzania.

Why this exists

Marine Protected Areas in the Western Indian Ocean are on the frontline of climate change. Rising sea temperatures, shifting ocean chemistry, accelerating sea level rise, and increasingly erratic rainfall are fundamentally altering the ecosystems MPAs are designed to protect, and the livelihoods of the communities that depend on them. Effective MPA management increasingly requires climate literacy: the ability to understand, interpret, and act on climate data.

Despite this exposure, climate change remains inadequately integrated into MPA management due to a lack of marine climate change indicators across the region. Management plans are largely built on historical baselines that no longer reflect current or near-future conditions, and the gap between available climate data and park managers' and rangers' practical knowledge remains wide. CliMPA exists specifically to bridge that gap.

Focus site: Mafia Island Marine Park

MIMP occupies a particularly exposed position within the Western Indian Ocean region. Located within the East African Coastal Current system, its reefs, seagrass meadows, and mangrove forests are subject to the compound pressures of warming, acidification, and sea-level rise, layered on top of longstanding local stressors including fishing pressure, sedimentation from the Rufiji River delta, and land-based pollution.
